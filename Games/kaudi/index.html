How to Play Kaudi

Objective

Be the first player or team to reach the target score (default 50 points) by tossing cowrie shells and scoring specific combinations based on how they land (mouth-up vs. mouth-down).

Scoring System (Standard 4 Shells)

In traditional Indian Kaudi, points are calculated based on the aperture (mouth) orientation:

  • 1 Mouth Up, 3 Down: 1 Point.
  • 2 Mouth Up, 2 Down: 2 Points (or Bust in Press-Your-Luck mode).
  • 3 Mouth Up, 1 Down: 3 Points.
  • 4 Mouth Up (All Up - Chhaka): 4 Points + Bonus Throw!
  • 0 Mouth Up (All Down - Ashta): 8 Points + Bonus Throw!

Game Modes

1. Classic Kaudi: Points add straight to your score. Scoring specials (All Up or All Down) earns an immediate bonus throw. First to cross the finish line wins!

2. Press-Your-Luck Mode: Each throw adds points to your round pot. You can Bank anytime to secure your points, or Toss Again to build the pot. Rolling zero points or a bust wipes out the unbanked pot!

Cultural Heritage of Kaudi

Derived from “An Anthology of Native & Indigenous Sports of India” published by Centurion University of Technology and Management (CUTM).

Ancient Roots & Currency

The use of cowrie shells (kaudi or kauri) dates back thousands of years across the Indian subcontinent. Historically, cowrie shells served as official currency, ornamental talismans, and sacred ceremonial objects.

Festivals & Folklore

References to cowrie games appear in classical sacred texts including the Bhagavata Purana. In Odisha, cowrie games are an indispensable custom during festivals such as Raja Parba and Laxmi Puja, where cowries are revered as sacred symbols of Goddess Lakshmi and harbingers of fortune and abundance.

The Heart of Indian Board Games

Beyond this standalone scoring contest, cowrie shells are the beating heart of India's classical race games — acting as the authentic randomizer for Ashtapada, Chauka Bara, Pachisi, Ashta Chamma, and Pasha Palli.
