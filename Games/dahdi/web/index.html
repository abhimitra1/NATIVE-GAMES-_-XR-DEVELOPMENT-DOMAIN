How to Play Dahdi

Line up three, take one.

Dahdi is an ancient Indian member of the Morris / Mills alignment games family, recorded in An Anthology of Native & Indigenous Sports of India (Centurion University, 2025).

1. Phase 1 — Placement

Players take turns placing their 9 pieces on any empty intersection on the three concentric squares. If you align three pieces in a straight line (a Mill / Dahdi), you immediately remove one opponent piece from the board.

Removal Rule: You cannot remove an enemy piece that is part of a formed mill, unless all enemy pieces are currently in mills.

2. Phase 2 — Movement

Once all 18 pieces are on the board, players slide one piece per turn to an adjacent empty point along the marked lines. Forming a new mill allows you to remove an enemy piece.

3. Phase 3 — Flying (Endgame)

When a player is reduced to only 3 pieces, their pieces gain the ability to "fly" — jumping to any empty point on the board.

Winning the Game

You win if:

  • You reduce the opponent to 2 pieces (unable to form a mill), or
  • The opponent has no legal moves (completely blocked/immobilized).
